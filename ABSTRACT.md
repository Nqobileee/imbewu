# An Offline AI-Powered Crop Disease Detection and Treatment Recommendation System for Smallholder Farmers in Zimbabwe

*Theme: Area 8 – Empowering SMEs with 4IR and AI for Inclusive and Sustainable Growth*
*Category: Prototype Demonstration*

## Abstract

Zimbabwe's agricultural sector supports 70% of the population, yet rural smallholder farmers continue to suffer significant crop losses to disease outbreaks, while existing digital advisory tools demand internet connectivity and target large-scale commercial operations—rendering them inaccessible to rural communities. This paper presents an offline, AI-powered mobile application that democratises crop disease diagnosis and treatment advice for rural Zimbabwean farmers. At its core is a locally trained convolutional neural network that performs on-device inference, allowing farmers to photograph affected crops and obtain a diagnosis without any network access. We demonstrate the diagnostic engine on Northern Corn Leaf Blight (NCLB) in maize, a major foliar disease, using a dataset of 1,821 field-collected leaf images in which the non-NCLB class consists of other maize diseases, requiring the model to recognise NCLB's characteristic tan, cigar-shaped lesions and distinguish them from other disease symptoms rather than merely separating diseased from healthy tissue. Built on a lightweight MobileNetV3 backbone suited to low-resource smartphones, the fine-tuned model correctly categorises 96.0% of held-out leaves (239 of 249) as NCLB or other-disease, achieving a ROC-AUC of 0.989 and a balanced accuracy of 0.959, with Grad-CAM analysis confirming lesion-focused predictions. From this diagnosis, the system recommends agrochemical and locally derived natural treatments with step-by-step guidance, delivered in English, Shona, and Ndebele. Aligned with Zimbabwe's National Artificial Intelligence Strategy (2026–2030) and advancing SDG 2 (Zero Hunger) and SDG 9 (Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure), it shows how offline-first Fourth Industrial Revolution technologies can strengthen food security in resource-constrained environments.
